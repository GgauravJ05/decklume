Does retrieval practice beat rereading?

01

The question

Retrieval practice

noun · also “the testing effect”

Learning by pulling information out of memory, rather than putting it back in by rereading.

“Closing the book and writing down what you remember is the practice.”

Prior work

A century of evidence, mostly in the lab

03

The results

Final test after one week

Retrieval practice scored higher

The retrieval group answered more questions correctly a week later.

Illustrative data (fictional study).

Effect size

Cohen’s d

d=Mretrieve−MrereadSDpooled

The difference between group means in units of their pooled standard deviation.

Effect size

0.84

A large effect by Cohen’s conventional benchmarks (0.8 and above).

Illustrative value (fictional study).

Hypothesis: practising recall improves long-term retention of course material.

Finding: supported, with a large effect after one week.

Limitations

What this study cannot tell us

  1. One course, one chapter. Other subjects may respond differently.
  2. One delay. We tested at one week, not across a term.
  3. Self-checked recall. Feedback quality varied between students.
