# Storyline

A deck is an argument delivered in a fixed order. Settle the argument before
any visual decision; design can't rescue a deck whose slides don't add up.

## Assertion headlines

Every content slide's headline is a **claim the slide proves**, written as a
short sentence. The body is the evidence.

| Topic label (weak) | Assertion (strong) |
|---|---|
| Sleep stages | Half the night is stage N2 |
| Q3 revenue | Revenue grew 63% on two new markets |
| Methodology | We tested 412 students across two terms |
| Competitors | Every rival charges per seat; we don't |

Test: read only the headlines in order. They should tell the whole story on
their own. Section, title, quote and statement slides are the exceptions.

## Arcs by purpose

**Lecture / class (20–50 min)**
Hook (a question or surprising fact) → agenda → for each concept: define →
explain → example → check understanding → recap → what to do next.
Use `l-define`, `l-steps`, `l-diagram`, `l-compare`, and a recap `l-list`.

**Pitch (10–15 slides)**
Problem → why now → solution → how it works → traction (`l-kpi`/`l-chart`) →
market → business model → competition (`l-compare`/`l-table`) → team
(`l-people`) → the ask (`l-closing`).

**Conference talk (speaker-led)**
Open with a story or a provocative `l-statement` → the idea in one sentence →
3 supporting points, each a short section with one example → the turn (what
changes) → close by repeating the one sentence.

**Research / thesis defense**
Question and why it matters → prior work (`l-timeline`) → gap → method
(`l-steps`/`l-diagram`) → results (`l-chart`, `l-table`, one claim per slide) →
limitations → contribution → future work. Reading mode suits committee
handouts; speaker-led suits the live defense.

**Status / business review (reading)**
Answer first (SCQA: situation, complication, question, answer) → evidence
grouped by theme → risks → decisions needed → next steps.

**Workshop / how-to**
Outcome → steps (`l-steps`) → each step with a worked example (`l-code`,
`l-split`) → common mistakes → practice task → resources.

## Pacing

- Speaker-led: about **1 slide per minute**. A 20-minute talk is 18–24 slides.
- Reading: fewer, fuller slides. A 10-minute read is 10–14 slides.
- Put a breathing slide (section, statement, KPI, quote or image) at least
  every 4–5 content slides.
- Never repeat the same layout more than twice in a row.
- The last slide carries the call to action or the one sentence to remember,
  not just "Questions?".

## Density limits

| | Speaker-led | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Words per slide (excluding notes) | ≤ 40 | ≤ 90 |
| Bullets | 3 | 5 |
| Cards | 3 | 4 |
| Table rows | 6 | 9 |
| Headline words | ≤ 8 | ≤ 12 |

Over the limit means **split into two slides**, with a "(continued)" eyebrow
if the headline repeats.

## Speaker notes

For speaker-led decks, write notes as what the speaker will actually say:
2–5 sentences, conversational, including the transition to the next slide.
The slide shows the claim; the notes carry the explanation.

## Builds and Magic Move, with intent

- Use `step` when the audience should consider points in order (an
  argument, a process). Don't build every list.
- Use `data-morph` for continuity: a number on a KPI slide that becomes one of
  four stats, a title that shrinks into a running header, an image that moves
  from full-bleed into a split. One or two per deck is enough.
